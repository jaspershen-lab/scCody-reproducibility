# Comparator methods: scNODE, scDiffEq and ARTEMIS

## Shared protocol

scNODE, scDiffEq and ARTEMIS were trained on the same AIDA expression data as scCody: the 2,000 highly variable genes selected on development donors, after CP10K normalization over the full gene set and log1p transformation. Each method was fitted three times, using the same donor splits as the three scCody fits. In every fit, all donors at six internal ages were withheld for checkpoint selection: ages 22, 30, 40, 50, 60 and 67 (fit 0); 23, 32, 42, 52, 62 and 66 (fit 1); and 24, 33, 43, 53, 63 and 65 (fit 2). All donors at the six external ages (21, 31, 41, 51, 61 and 68; 71 donors) were withheld for evaluation. The remaining 486, 502 and 477 donors in fits 0, 1 and 2 formed the training sets and also supplied the source cells at evaluation. Fits 0, 1 and 2 used random seeds 42, 43 and 44.

**Checkpoint selection.** Checkpoints were chosen by the internal-holdout criterion used for scCody. All training cells at the youngest training age (19 years) were pooled and simulated forward to the chronological age of each internal holdout donor. For each donor (at most 80 donors with at least 100 cells each, and at most 256 cells per donor), the model generated as many cells as the donor contributed. The Sinkhorn divergence between generated and observed cells was then computed in log-expression space (blur 0.05, scaling 0.5). The checkpoint with the lowest donor-averaged divergence was retained. Core scCody, all three comparators and HPO use `sccody.selection` for this score and pooled youngest-age source sampling. Target-cell sampling uses the supplied seed; ties retain the earlier epoch. Only the final selected metric, epoch/phase and score are recorded. scNODE was scored after each of 50 epochs, scDiffEq every 25 of 500 epochs, and ARTEMIS after each of 20 training phases. The selected checkpoints were epochs 5, 6 and 11 for scNODE, epochs 500, 500 and 475 for scDiffEq, and phases 19, 17 and 16 for ARTEMIS (fits 0, 1 and 2). External donors were never used for training or selection.

**Age localization.** Each external donor with at least 100 cells was localized separately. Its source populations were training cells from donors of the same sex at fixed source ages, which were chosen without reference to any target: 19, 33, 44, 55 and 66 years (fit 0); 19, 33, 44, 55 and 67 (fit 1); and 19, 32, 44, 55 and 67 (fit 2). For each source age, up to 2,000 target cells and the same number of source cells were sampled. The source cells were simulated to every candidate age on a 1-year grid. The estimate for that source was the candidate age that minimized the Sinkhorn divergence between simulated and observed cells in the method's own latent space (at most 2,000 cells, blur 0.05, scaling 0.5). Exact ties were broken at random. The donor's predicted age was the unweighted mean of its per-source estimates, and was reported only when every source produced one. Predictions were averaged over the three fits.

The methods differed in direction of simulation and age range. scNODE integrated each source both forward and backward in time over candidate ages 19–77. ARTEMIS sampled its learned forward process for ages above the source and its learned backward process for ages below it. Its source and candidate ages were limited to 19–77. scDiffEq learns only a forward-time stochastic process, so it was simulated forward from the 19-year source alone, over candidate ages 20–77.

**Cell-type localization.** For lineage-level estimates, the global source populations and simulations were reused. Simulated cells at each candidate age were assigned to T, myeloid, B, NK or dendritic cell lineages by a k-nearest-neighbour classifier on principal components, fitted only on training cells. Each lineage was then localized separately against the matching target cells. DC localization requires at least five target cells across all models, including the pooled PCA-KNN readout and the reports that filter to DC before simulation. The pooled readout also requires five generated DC cells at a candidate age; other lineages retain their 100-cell minimum. The bundled tables remain the saved results; these rules apply when reports are regenerated.

**Generated populations.** For population reconstruction and differential-expression recovery, up to 2,000 observed cells were sampled at each external age and split by sex. For each sex, the same number of cells was generated by simulating the sex-matched source populations directly to the target age, with cells divided equally across source ages. Generated and observed cells were compared on expression, distribution and donor-pseudobulk edgeR differential expression. Differential expression was computed against a reference of all AIDA cells outside the six external ages, stratified by age, cell type and sex (at most 50,000 cells).

## scNODE

scNODE was implemented from the published scNODE architecture. The model was a variational autoencoder with a 50-dimensional latent space, a linear encoder with hidden layers of 50 and 50 units, and a single-layer linear decoder. Latent dynamics were described by an ordinary differential equation whose drift was a ReLU network with hidden layers of 50 and 50 units. Model time is rescaled linearly to [0, 1] over the fitted training-age range, recorded with the checkpoint.

The ODE was integrated with the explicit Euler method, with steps placed only at the requested time points. During training these were the observed training ages. During localization they were the 1-year candidate grid. For generated populations and checkpoint selection, a single step was taken from source age to target age.

The autoencoder was first pretrained for 600 iterations on reconstruction mean squared error. Each batch contained 2,048 cells, split across ages in proportion to their cell counts. The full model was then trained for 50 epochs of 200 iterations, each simulating 200 cells from the youngest age through all training ages. The loss was the Sinkhorn divergence to 200 observed cells per age in expression space, plus 0.197 times the corresponding divergence in latent space. Both stages used Adam (learning rate 10⁻³, β = (0.95, 0.99)).

## scDiffEq

scDiffEq version 1.1.0 was used through its published interface. Expression was standardized and projected onto 50 principal components fitted on training cells. The stochastic differential equation was learned in this space, and generated cells were mapped back to expression and clipped at zero.

The model used scDiffEq's default time-invariant potential drift with velocity-ratio regularization (target ratio 1.0). The drift network had two hidden layers of 2,048 units and the diffusion network two hidden layers of 128 units. It was trained for 500 epochs with a learning rate of 3 × 10⁻⁴. Model time is rescaled to [0, 1] over the fitted training-age range, recorded with the checkpoint. The integration step size was 1.0 on this axis. Each simulation was therefore a single Euler–Maruyama step from the source time to the final requested time, with intermediate times obtained by linear interpolation.

## ARTEMIS

The published ARTEMIS implementation was used unchanged. Expression was first encoded by a separate variational autoencoder with a 50-dimensional latent space and one hidden layer of 512 ReLU units in both the encoder and the decoder. It was trained for 100 epochs (batch size 64, Adam learning rate 10⁻³) on mean squared reconstruction error plus 10⁻³ times the Kullback–Leibler divergence. Cells were embedded at the posterior mean, and simulated latent states were decoded with the same network.

Model time was age divided by 100, discretized into 100 steps of one year each. ARTEMIS's forward and backward score networks and its birth–death network were trained by the divergence objective with the published defaults otherwise. Training ran for 20 phases, each consisting of 10 forward–backward simulation passes, with batch size 512 and learning rate 10⁻³. During simulation, cells removed by the learned death process were excluded. All simulated cells were used only when fewer than two survived.

Population reconstruction uses the shared target-cell sampler in `sccody_analyses.population_inputs`. Standard report profiles export prediction arrays and PCA/KNN lineage estimates; the `*_dc_report.args` profiles supply the DC filter-first estimates for Figure 3. Run these reports before `sccody_analyses.collect_figure3`.
